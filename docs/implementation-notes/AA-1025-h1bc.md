# AA-1025 slices H1b + H1c, CIS side (S265 lane l1-h1bc) — the other apps' app profiles

Plan: root `docs/audits/2026-11-01-S263-gateway-gap-audit/plan.md` row H1 (H1a: `AA-1025-d3-h1a.md`). TripPlanner
(`AA-TripPlanner-Web docs/implementation-notes/AA-1025-h1b.md`) and the CRM (`AA-Partner
docs/implementation-notes/AA-1025-h1c.md`) act on their profile; this note is what CIS adds. $0.

## Decisions

- **Migration 347 (contract, migration-only PR)**: `catalog.v_llm_app_profile_v1`, the `tripplanner` row only, six
  columns (`app`, `ai_enabled`, `bulk_paused`, `default_daily_budget_usd`, `degrade_mode`, `updated_at`); not the
  admin's free-text reason, not `updated_by`. Column SELECT for `catalog_owner`, view SELECT for `tripplanner` only, the
  X1 S22 catalog recipe (as 323 / 329 / 341). Contract because of the column GRANT on an existing table and the recipe.
  Its precondition is 323 (the contract views and recipe in place) and 345.
- **The TripPlanner spend signal reaches CIS through the ledger.** TripPlanner prints `llm_spend_alert` lines in its
  own CloudWatch log; a metric filter + alarm is paid and outside the in-app alert diet. Instead the ops check reads
  the same two facts TripPlanner decides on (its profile budget and its `shared.llm_call_log` rows today, the
  `COALESCE(app, quality_signal->>'app')` rule of CIS 323), so the bell says exactly what TripPlanner's line says.
  Two checks per app, `llm_spend_alert.<app>.near` and `.cap`, so a cap reached hours after the near alert is not
  swallowed by the 6 h bell throttle; at most one e-mail per check per UTC day (the S263 rule).
- **Only TripPlanner in `APP_SPEND_APPS`.** CIS's own cap is `llm/app:cis` (A7, alerts from the gateway); the CRM's
  spend is in `crm.llm_call` (ADR 0008a), which CIS reads only with a staff token, and the sampler has none. The
  CRM signal is the CRM's own ops check instead (fix pass after review): its job sampler e-mails the platform alerts
  topic, the topic of this module's e-mail, at 80 % / 100 % of its daily budget (`llm_app_spend_near` / `_cap`,
  AA-Partner `docs/implementation-notes/AA-1025-h1c.md`); no CIS bell for it (that needs an M2M client for
  crm.ops.v1, a separate decision).
- **No profile budget = no check.** CIS cannot see TripPlanner's env cap (`TP_DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD`); setting the
  TripPlanner budget on the card makes it the cap of both sides.
- **The CRM relay (H1c)**: `PATCH /admin/ai-gateway/app-profiles/crm` validates the edit (`app_profile.merged`), relays
  the whole profile to `crm.ops.v1` `PUT /llm/app-profile` with the staff member's own WORKFORCE token
  (`crm_console.crm_ops_call`, the path added to `ALLOWED`), and only after a 200 updates and audits the CIS row. A CRM
  refusal answers 401 / 403 / 422 / 503 as the CRM said (else 502) and changes nothing in CIS. Relay first, not CIS
  first: a CIS row that says "off" while the CRM still runs would be a lie on the card.
- **"Takes effect"** (`enforced_here`): true for cis, tripplanner, crm; Booking makes no AI call. The copy changed to
  "Now, in the app's own gateway (TripPlanner within 20 s)" / "Not used (the app makes no AI call)".

## Tradeoffs

- The check reads today's ledger rows of one app every 5 minutes (`created_at` index range, a few hundred rows a day on
  Dev): cheap, and it runs only when a budget is set.
- Relay then update is not one transaction: if the CIS update fails after the CRM took the profile, the CRM is ahead
  until the next edit (rare: the same values were validated by the same rules first).
