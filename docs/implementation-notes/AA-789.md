# AA-789 — T10 gate recall tuning (Report 12 §1.3)

S257 (29/10/2026). The S234 panel (rounds r2–r4) found defect classes the deterministic T10 gates did not catch
(root `docs/audits/2026-10-13-S234-t-series-rounds/12-gates-t2-tenants.md` §1.3). §1.3 is a table of classes with
no sentences; the examples come from the round reports 03 (r2), 04 (r3) and 07 (r4). This change widens the patterns
behind existing gates only. No severity flag changes and no new gate is added (standing rule since S246: no new
blocking gates for 4 weeks).

Revision 2 (same session): an independent verifier refuted revision 1 with clean travel sentences that the widened
patterns flagged, fixtures without provenance, and the point that every widened pattern feeds a gate that already
blocks and can hold. Revision 2 narrows every pattern so none of the verifier's clean sentences fire (all are now
negative tests), and drops the patterns that could not be made precise (listed under Changed).

Revision 3 (same session): a second independent reviewer (fix_then_ship) found 8 more clean sentences that revision 2
flagged under blocking gates (two naming-note terms, three predicate repeats, a hedge on a list, freight 'by boat', a
side place called optional, a clause-crossing 'lunch … in Mörön', a safety caveat 'hard to overstate', and two
'technical' route facts). Each is fixed below and is now a negative test; 'hard to overstate' is dropped.

## Decisions
- **Precision first.** F13_leak, F13_repeat, F17_trip_fact and F18_rarity block and can hold (`never_holds` exempts
  only the style gates and F14). A false positive under them can hold a good piece when the sentence repair keeps the
  flagged wording. So a pattern stays only if it needs the words of the claim itself, not just the sentence shape.
- **F15_voice, contrast frames** (`hygiene.CONTRAST_FRAME_RES`, indices 8–9 appended, so earlier indices keep their
  meaning; `quality_gates._CONTRAST_RANK` ranks them):
  - 8, the reversed clause-final `Y, not X.`, only when X is an abstraction: an `-al/-ic/-ive/-ous/-ful` adjective on
    an abstract noun (`-ment/-ence/-ion/-ity …`: r4 #8 'institutional, not ceremonial sentiment', r4 class 4 'a
    founding argument, not a scenic preference'), something 'frozen in / for' (r4 #1 'not a site frozen for
    visitors'), or a stock dismissal noun ('museum', 'postcard', 'accident' …). Lower-case words only (no proper
    nouns), no numbers.
  - 9, the broetry pair: a short `Not …` fragment answered by a two-word fragment ending on the same word (r3 #15
    'Not squeezed in. Planned in.'). A lone `Not …` fragment ('Not suitable for children under seven.') is copy.
  - The density rule (more than 1 frame per 150 words) still decides whether a piece gets a finding.
- **F15_voice, a frame inside a stock-quoted sentence** (`_in_stock_sentence`) neither counts toward the density nor
  gets a second finding: the stock repair rewrites that sentence anyway, and a second overlapping finding widened the
  sentence-repair span (existing test `test_not_as_pair_is_one_quoted_span`).
- **F15 stock lines in blogs**: `channel_spec.CROSS_CHANNEL_STOCK_PATTERNS` is a separate, narrower copy of two
  AA-780 lines, read by `hygiene.TEMPLATE_LINE_RES` on every channel: 'does not perform' only at a clause end (with an
  optional 'for visitors / you …'), 'tolerates' only with a pronoun ('them', 'you', 'us'). The panel found exactly
  these in blogs (r4 #15, #17). `STOCK_PATTERNS` (LinkedIn) is back to its `main` content.
- **F17_trip_fact**:
  - Hedge scope per list item (`_item_hedged`): a hedge covers its own item and the items after it, never an earlier
    one, except a collective predicate in the last item. r4 #14 put the boat between 'on foot' and 'optionally on
    horseback'; the old whole-clause hedge let it through. Collective (`_COLLECTIVE_HEDGE_RE`, revision 3): a copula
    predicate ('are all optional', 'are available on request', 'is at extra cost'), a modal passive ('can be arranged /
    booked / added …', reviewer 'Boat rides, horse riding and visits to nomadic families can be arranged on
    request.') or 'cost(s) extra'. A verbless 'on request' / 'at extra cost' is the item's own hedge ('…, with horse
    riding on request' still plans the boat). A collective predicate never covers an item that is a statement of its
    own (travellers + travel verb: 'Families travel by boat, and horse riding can be arranged').
  - Travelling by the vehicle an optional activity names counts as mentioning it (`_MODE_RE`: 'by boat'), but only
    with the travellers as the subject of a travel verb in the clause (families / you / we / travellers / the group …
    + cross / travel / explore / reach …) or a sentence that states the plan ('… are part of how this stage of the
    journey unfolds', r4 #5, whose dashes make 'by boat on the water' a clause of its own). Reviewer 'Supplies for
    the lakeside camps still arrive by boat in summer.' is freight and stays clean.
  - Reverse flip (`included_as_optional`, r4 #18): only three shapes whose subject is the visit itself: 'an optional
    day / visit / excursion … to / at / with <X>', a clause opening on 'Visiting <X> / A visit to <X> / The day with
    <X>' that ends in 'is optional', and '<X> visit / day is optional' at the clause start. Revision 3: <X> must BE the
    planned moment, every word of it a word of the moment's name or Naming note (`_object_is_moment`), so a side place
    near the moment is never read (reviewer 'optional excursions to the mineral spring' at Sod Camp, 'An optional stop
    at Uran Togoo's crater rim viewpoint'). A sub-activity of a planned visit may be optional, so 'if they wish',
    'add-on', 'not included' and a bare 'optional' are never read.
  - Time idioms (`_TIME_IDIOM_RE`): 'worn out by midday' (r4 #11 false positive), 'at the end of the day' … are not
    schedules.
  - `PACE_RE` gains only the negated difficulty promise 'not (a) / no technical <climb | trek | hike | route …>' (r4
    #14 'not a technical climb'), never when the next word is gear / equipment / kit / skills, never an un-negated
    'technical' (reviewer 'No technical climbing gear is needed', 'the short technical scramble below the summit').
    Blog-only like the rest of the pace check (AA-775); a seed-backed word passes.
  - `near_as_at`: a meal / stop the source puts NEAR a place, written AT / IN it (r4 fix 2 'lunch at X'; r3 'A stop in
    Mörön'). Revision 3: only the tight shape '<stop word> (is | was)? (at | in) <place>'; the old 40-character window
    crossed clauses (reviewer 'After lunch, the drive continues to the airport in Mörön'). A negated stop ('does not
    stop in Mörön') is no claim.
- **F18_rarity**: 'unlike almost anything', 'anything else on earth', '(most of | the rest of) the world has never /
  rarely …', 'a significant / substantial … fraction / share of the world / global …'. A
  superlative with no scope ('one of the most coherent …') is backed only by the same superlative word in the source,
  no longer by any superlative there.
- **F13_leak, naming note** (`naming_note_leaks`, revision 3; `LEAK_RE` is back to its `main` content): 'phonetic
  approximation' (r3 #14) only in a sentence that names a place whose moment has a seed `Naming:` note (by any spelling
  the note gives), and 'correctly spelled' only with such a name right beside it ('is correctly spelled Mörön',
  'Murun is correctly spelled …'). Reviewer 'Make sure your name is correctly spelled on the visa application' and
  'The Dzongkha greeting kuzuzangpo is written here as a phonetic approximation.' stay clean. Reported by
  `find_issues` (sentence repair); autofix never trims on it (it is not in `LEAK_RE`).
- **F13_leak, brief wording**: `BRIEF_WORDING_RE` is the brief's 'one X, one Y, one Z' recipe as a sentence of its own whose last item is a
  brief word (fact, detail, date …): r2 #19 'One ruined site, one date, one fact.'. Read by `find_issues` only
  (sentence repair), never by autofix.
- **F13_repeat**: `paraphrased_repeats` reads predicates only: both sentences describe their subject as '(is |
  remains | serves as | functions as …) a <w1> <w2> <noun>' with the same w1 w2 (w2 an adjective ending), a
  different noun, w1 w2 not in the source, within 4 sentences, and the SAME subject (r4 #1 'is still a living
  religious seat' / 'continues to function as a living religious institution'). Revision 3 (`_same_subject`,
  `_antecedent_is`): the later subject's head noun is the earlier one's and its other words a subset of the earlier
  subject's, determiners and ordinals included ('The dzong' after 'Punakha Dzong'; never 'Our day-four lodge' after
  'Our day-two lodge' nor 'The second camp' after 'The first camp'), or the later subject is a pronoun whose nearest
  antecedent of the same number is that subject. A predicate with 'also' / 'too' / 'as well' adds a fact (reviewer
  'It is also a working religious school') and is never read. Reported, never deleted by autofix.

## Changed
- The issue asked for "F17 reverse flips + idioms"; the 'technical' difficulty promise and 'lunch at vs near' (same
  §1.3 row, r4 fix 2) are in too.
- 'Digits vs words' and 'hashtag near-miss' (§1.3, "new checks") are out of scope: they are new checks, not recall
  tuning.
- **Dropped in revision 2** (could not be made precise; each panel example is pinned as a known miss in the tests):
  - Contrast frame 'does not … ; it …' (verb negation answered after `;` / `:`): it fired on 'The camp does not have
    Wi-Fi; it does have a generator'. The panel's example (r4 #15) is two sentences, 'does not perform for visitors.
    It simply continues.', and the stock line already catches it.
  - Contrast frame 'less X, more Y': no panel example (the revision-1 fixture was invented); it fired on 'Expect less
    driving, more walking'.
  - A lone 'Not …' fragment: it fired on 'Not suitable for children under seven.'. Only the pair shape stays.
  - F13_leak 'two named rivers' (r3 #7): in both stored pieces the names follow ('between two named rivers — the Male
    River and the Female River'), the same shape as the clean 'The two named passes on the road, Dochula and Pele La,
    both have prayer flags.'.
  - F13_leak 'loose / common approximation', 'best avoided', 'should be referred to as': read as plain copy (a trail
    map, a distance, the midday sun, a courtesy tip).
  - F13_repeat `subjectless_section_openers` (r4 #16 'Access requires a multi-hour horse or reindeer ride.'): its
    heading ('Into the Taiga: The Tsaatan Reindeer Herders') names what the access is to, exactly as in the clean
    '## Punakha Dzong / Entry requires modest dress.'; the text cannot tell the panel's orphan from plain copy.
  - F16 `off_attribute` (weather / purpose / when questions): it dropped real answers ('The Tsaatan ride their
    reindeer and milk them', '−10 °C at night', 'clear skies and crisp nights', a correct cash-only answer to a
    yes/no question), and each drop costs an FAQ regeneration. The real r4 #19 answer ('… do not primarily raise them
    for meat.') is already caught by `negative_only` on `main`, and the r4 #17 weather answer was truncated, not off
    topic. 'When' had no panel example.
  - F17 'strenuous', 'gruelling' (safety caveats: 'The hike is strenuous in places, so good boots matter.') and
    'effortless' (no panel example).
  - F18 bare 'most of the world' ('Travellers from most of the world can get a visa') and a bare 'significant /
    considerable share' ('A considerable share of the walk is on flat ground').
  - F18 'hard / difficult / impossible to overstate' (r4 #4, revision 3): as often a safety caveat ('The importance of
    sun protection at this altitude is hard to overstate.'), and telling a place figure from advice needs more than
    the words. Pinned as a known miss.
  - F13_leak 'phonetic approximation' / 'correctly spelled' as bare `LEAK_RE` terms (revision 3): now naming-note
    bound (see Decisions).

## Tradeoffs
- Recall vs a false hold: the dropped patterns lose 4 panel examples (r4 #16 orphan opener, r3 #7 'two named
  rivers', r1 #0 'river symbolism', r4 #4 'hard to overstate'). The revision-3 narrowing also misses a pronoun repeat
  when a sentence with another subject of the same number sits between the two ('It' after 'Dinner is at seven'), and
  'by boat' with no traveller subject and no plan statement. They stay with the reviewer and the planned post-repair integrity check (§1.4).
- Pattern 8 still fires on a structural 'Y, not X' with abstract words that is a plain contrast ('a local
  celebration, not a national competition'). It is F15 (blocks on attempt 1 only, never holds) and counts only above
  1 frame per 150 words, so a single one never produces a finding.
- More F15 / F13 findings mean more cheap sentence repairs on attempt 1 (about $0.012 each, Report 12 §1.1).
