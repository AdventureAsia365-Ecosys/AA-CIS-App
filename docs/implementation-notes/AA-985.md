# AA-985 — F17_trip_fact "ended role": role identity, not place identity

Lane `aa985-f17`, session S248. Trigger: blog piece 4b58f52c (Bhutan, request 3c9dc7bb) was held on
F17_trip_fact, "says it still holds a role the source dates as ended (the capital and seat of government of
Bhutan, until 1955)". The flagged sentence was "Like all dzongs, it houses both monk bodies and government offices
for district administration". The seed dates only the national role as ended. The district administration the
dzong houses is a different role, and the dzong still holds it.

## Root cause

`trip_facts.date_authority_claims` decided "this clause claims the ended role" with `_gov_claim`:

- `_LOCAL_GOV_RE` removed a local governance phrase only in the form `<local unit> [word] <gov word>`
  ("district government offices"). The flagged sentence has the reverse order: "government offices **for** district
  administration". The regex removed "district administration" and left "government offices".
- "government" was a *strong* term, so it counted as the ended role with no national scope. Together with
  the present-tense verb "houses", it fired.

So the rule asked whether this place still holds a governmental function. It should ask whether the place still
holds the same role the seed dates as ended. This is place identity versus role identity.

## Decisions

- **New public `trip_facts.role_claim(clause, country, role)`** replaces the private `_gov_claim`. It is the
  single role-identity decision, and the census script and tests use it too.
  - **Local phrases are removed, not used to excuse the clause.** `_strip_other_roles` removes every local
    governance phrase in either order, then decides on what is left. The two orders are `_LOCAL_GOV_RE`
    ("district government offices") and the new `_LOCAL_OF_RE` ("government offices for district
    administration", "seat of government for the district", "administrative centre of Punakha District").
    A strong term left beside the local phrase still fires ("It remains the capital and the district seat").
    In `_LOCAL_OF_RE` only a capitalised proper noun may sit between "of/for" and the unit
    (`(?-i:[A-Z])`), so "seat of government and district …" is not a local phrase. A local phrase coordinated
    with the national scope stays ("the government of the district and the kingdom").
  - **Hosted government functions are weak** (`_GOV_HOSTED_RE`): "government offices / officials /
    departments / staff / bodies …". Ministries and agencies are not on the list: hosting
    the ministries is being the seat of government. A building that hosts offices does not claim to be the seat
    of government. These count only with a national scope ("Bhutan's government offices", "the national
    government offices").
  - **A non-governmental "capital" is a different role** (`_OTHER_CAPITAL_RE`): spiritual, cultural, religious,
    monastic, culinary, trekking … capital.
  - Kept as strong (always the ended national role): "capital", "seat of government / power", bare
    "government(al)". "The founding purpose, religious and governmental in one, remains present" still fires,
    which matches the existing S234 test.
  - **A local ended role** works the other way round. Example: "served as the district administrative centre
    until 1990". A clause that names a local governance role is that role. Before this change the local strip
    made such a role impossible to catch.
- **The span check is gated on the role too** (`_span_about_role`), on the same stripped text. A span sentence
  only about the local role ("For nearly four centuries it has housed the district administration") is no longer
  checked against the national role's 1637–1955 dates. A span sentence that still names the role after the strip
  ("For nearly three centuries it served as the capital and district seat") is checked as before.

## Tradeoffs

- **Only the local phrase itself is excused.** "It remains the seat of government for the district" passes,
  because the whole governance phrase is bound to the district. That sentence is true of Punakha today.
  `_CLAUSE_SPLIT_RE` does not split on a bare " and ", so mixed sentences are judged as one clause: the local
  phrase is removed and any strong term left still fires. The first cut used an early return ("local phrase and
  no national scope → not a claim"); review round 1 showed it lost true positives the old rule caught, so it was
  removed.
- **The "other capital" list is a closed word list.** The S245 lesson (AA-779) says not to keep widening a
  regex list on single cases. This one is small and closed, and new cases should come from labelled hits in
  the census and the calibration loop (root memo, docs/audits/2026-10-23-S248-calibration-loop), not from
  ad-hoc additions.
- Bare "government" stays strong to keep the S234 true positive. "Government" with no national scope and no
  local scope still fires, so "it remains a centre of government" is still flagged.

## Review round 1 (S248)

An independent review found four gaps; all fixed in a follow-up commit, each with tests that fail on the first
commit:

1. Blocker: the early return `if local_scoped and not national: return False` dropped true positives the old
   rule caught ("It remains the capital and the district seat.", "It is still the capital, seat of the regional
   government." …). Removed; the decision is made on the stripped text only.
2. `_LOCAL_OF_RE` let any word stand in for a proper noun (the pattern is compiled with `re.I`), so
   "seat of government and district" was a local phrase. Now `(?-i:[A-Z])`. A local phrase followed by
   "and <national scope>" is also kept ("the government of the district and the kingdom").
3. `_span_about_role` skipped any sentence with a local phrase. It now decides on the stripped text, so
   "For nearly three centuries it served as the capital and district seat" is checked again.
4. `_GOV_HOSTED_RE` no longer lists ministries / ministers / agencies: "It still houses government ministries"
   fires again.

## Round 2 (S248): entity binding

Trigger: the read-only census after #955 deployed (`dev-34730387`). It found 20 stored hits from 4 pieces of the
Bhutan request 3c9dc7bb. #955's matcher still fired on 13 of them, which are 12 distinct sentences. All 20 are false
positives (reviewed one by one; none of them says Punakha Dzong is still the capital or seat of government):

- class statements: "A dzong is a fortress-monastery that functions … as the administrative headquarters … of a
  Bhutanese district", "In Bhutan, dzongs house government offices alongside monk bodies", "Like all dzongs, it
  houses …", "They house …", "Their dual administrative and religious function …";
- other places: "Kuenselphodrang, on a forested hillside above the capital" (the capital there is Thimphu),
  "Khaling, where the National Handloom Development Corporation operates a government-run weaving centre",
  "Mongar Dzong houses …";
- other subjects: "The weavers at the government looms in Khaling still produce kishuthara".

Root cause class: **entity binding**. #955 asked *which* role a clause claims, but not *whose*. A clause with
government vocabulary and a present verb counted as a claim by the entity, whatever its subject was.

### Decisions

- **`EndedRole` names the entity** (`entity`, `aliases`): the proper-noun subject of the seed line that dates the
  role ("Punakha Dzong served as … until 1955"). When that line opens on a pronoun, the entity is the moment itself
  (label name, first part of `Location:`, quoted `Naming:` names). Aliases add the name without its place-kind head
  ("Punakha"), so "Punakha remains the seat of government" still fires. `Moment` now keeps its `location` /
  `naming` lines.
- **`binds_entity(clause, preceding, heading, binder)`** is the new gate, applied after `role_claim` + tense. It
  decides on the clause's subject:
  - the entity's name or alias → bound; any other proper name (Mongar Dzong, Kuenselphodrang, Thimphu) → not;
  - a plural / indefinite / class subject ("dzongs", "a dzong", "they", "these fortresses", "this building
    type") and any class comparison ("Like all dzongs, …", "as the older western dzongs do", "in active dzongs")
    → never ("Unlike other dzongs, …" is a contrast and is not a class marker);
  - a singular pronoun or definite NP ("it", "its role", "the dzong", "the founding purpose") → its antecedent:
    the last place named before it in the same paragraph (earlier clauses of the same sentence included; "the
    dzong" prefers a name ending in "Dzong"), then the section heading, then, with nothing named at all, the
    entity (fail closed: a lone "It is still the capital of Bhutan." still fires);
  - the government itself as subject ("Bhutan's government still meets …", "Here the national government …")
    → bound only by a reference to the entity (its name, "here", "inside it", "within its walls") or as an
    appositive predicate ("Punakha Dzong, still the seat of Bhutan's government");
  - relative pieces get their own subject: ", which …" binds to the name right before the comma ("We reach
    Punakha Dzong, which is still the seat …" fires; "climbs to the capital, which is still …" does not);
    ", where …" uses its own subject.
  - A claim inside a complement clause ("Visitors rarely realise it is still the seat of government") resolves the
    embedded "it".
- **Role identity, two more phrases** (`_strip_other_roles`): "the capital" after a spatial preposition ("above the
  capital", "a drive from the capital") is a place on the map, not a role; "government-run / -owned / -funded …"
  names who runs something else.
- The span check uses the same binding ("For nearly three centuries Trongsa Dzong served as …" is no longer
  checked against Punakha's 1637–1955).
- Still deterministic: no LLM call, no DB.

### Tradeoffs

- **A small rule-based subject finder, not a parser.** The categories are closed word lists (place kinds,
  relational nouns, government heads, lead prepositions). New cases should come from labelled census hits and the
  calibration loop, not from ad-hoc additions (same rule as round 1).
- **The unresolved default is the entity.** Single-sentence inputs (unit tests, the census, a meta) have no
  antecedent; the rule keeps catching "It remains the seat of government." there. The cost: an orphan "it" in a
  paragraph about another place without a name or heading still fires. That is the old behaviour, not a new risk.
- **Class statements never fire, by spec.** "Like all dzongs, Punakha Dzong still houses the seat of government"
  is silenced too. Writers do not produce that shape (0 of 20 hits); a contrast ("Unlike other dzongs") still fires.
- "these walls still house the seat of government" (plural NP meaning a part of the entity) is treated as plural
  and does not fire.

### Review round (round 2)

An independent review of the round 2 commit found seven gaps. Each is a set of sentences that either fired on
something other than the entity, or went silent on a real claim that #955 still caught. Every one of those sentences
is now a test in `tests/unit/test_aa985_f17_entity_binding_review.py`.

Decisions:

1. **A government subject is bound by a locative relation, not by its verb.** The `_GOV_VERB_RE` whitelist and
   `_LOCATIVE_RE` are gone. `_gov_binding` binds "the government / the capital / the seat of government" as
   subject only when:
   - a locative lead places it: "Here …", "Inside, …", "Inside Punakha Dzong …";
   - a place comes right after the verb: "meets inside Punakha Dzong", "is housed in the dzong", "sits here";
   - the place is the object of "occupies / fills" or of a copula: "occupies the dzong", "is still Punakha
     Dzong";
   - "from <place>" appears anywhere: "governs Bhutan from the dzong / from these halls".

   What the government does to or at the place stays silent: "maintains the dzong", "sponsors festivals at Punakha
   Dzong", "restricts photography inside", "requires … here". With no finite verb after the subject at all, the
   clause is an appositive or a bullet, and the antecedent decides.
   A definite NP now ends at its government head, so "the national government works from …" is a government
   subject.
2. **A paragraph's other places include plain one-word subjects.** `_antecedents` adds the capitalised subject
   that opens each preceding sentence or clause ("Hanoi lies …", "Vientiane sits …") as an OTHER mention. It skips:
   - an alias or the country;
   - a COMMON_CAPS word, a government word or a place-kind word;
   - a clause that still talks about "it" ("Zhabdrung founded it in 1637": an agent, and the topic carries on).

   `_resolve` takes the last sentence or clause that names a place. In it, a known place standing as the subject
   wins (the entity, the successor, a seed moment, a "<Name> Dzong"), so "Tokyo replaced Kyoto in 1869. It is …"
   is Tokyo. Otherwise the last name wins, so "Zhabdrung … founded Punakha Dzong. It …" is the dzong.
   `_SUCCESSOR_RE` also reads "when X became the capital", "replaced by X" and "the capital is now X".
3. **", still the …" is an appositive.** `_clause_units` passes the clause before such a split to
   `binds_entity(appositive_np=…)`. The binding is the name right before the comma ("Punakha Dzong, still the
   capital of Bhutan, sits …"). After a full clause ("Punakha Dzong sits where two rivers meet, still the seat
   …"), it is that clause's subject.
4. **The moment's own names are the entity when the role line names the moment or its city.** Example: "Hue
   served as …", where the moment is "Imperial City" with Location "Imperial City, Hue, …". In that case
   `_role_entity` adds the moment's label, first Location part and Naming aliases to `aliases`, and `_binder`
   no longer lists them as other places. A lone generic adjective left after dropping the place-kind head
   ("Imperial" of "Imperial City") is not an alias. "The Imperial Palace in Kyoto" and "The Royal Palace of
   Luang Prabang" bind through "in / of <alias>" after the named head.
5. **A contrast or adjunct lead is never the antecedent.** A pronoun subject resolves against the text before
   the clause only, not against the stripped lead ("Unlike Mongar Dzong, it …", "After visiting Mongar Dzong,
   you will see it …"). The successor skip ignores a contrast lead, so "Unlike Thimphu, it is still the capital of
   Bhutan." now fires (#955 missed it).
6. **Aliases match blind to case and accents.** They match on `fold()`ed text through a character index map
   (`_fold_map`, LRU-cached), so "Huế", "Kyōto", "PUNAKHA DZONG" and "punakha dzong" all match. Two guards:
   - A one-word alias in lower case counts only in all-lower-case text, so "the hue of the river" is not Hue.
   - An alias run on into another capitalised word is a different name: "Punakha Tshechu", "Punakha Valley".
7. **Naming aliases count with a proper-noun role subject** (folded through decision 4).

Tradeoffs and limits:

- This is still a small rule-based reader. Where it is uncertain it now prefers the structure (locative relation,
  subject position) over word lists. Verbs are no longer listed; only the occupancy verbs ("occupies / fills")
  and the copula are named, as the review allowed.
- "Bhutan's government still holds its winter sessions here" is silent, because the locative comes after an
  object. It has the same shape as the reviewer's false positive "requires visitors to wear formal dress here".
- With no successor in the seed, "Hanoi lies 700 km north of Hue. It is the capital today" still fires: the
  unknown subject "Hanoi" loses to the later known "Hue". A seed with "replaced by Hanoi" makes Hanoi known and
  silences it.
- A one-word proper noun with "it" in its clause is read as an agent ("Hanoi's streets … it …" will not count
  Hanoi).
