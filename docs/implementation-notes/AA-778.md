# AA-778: Em-dash. Deterministic autofix, strip em-dashes from our prompts, then a residue guard

Plan: AA-Ecosys `docs/audits/2026-10-13-S234-t-series-rounds/09-next-step-plan.md` #5 and §3.1 (first bullet).
Offline check and before / after sample: `docs/implementation-notes/AA-778-offline-check.md`.

## Decisions

- **One step, `hygiene.fix_dashes(text, ctx=None)`, called inside `hygiene.autofix`** after every sentence
  deletion (trip name, borrowed / leak / social-proof deletes, dedupe, fix_misplaced) and before the hashtag
  layout and first-line split. The deletion rules compare sentences as the writer wrote them; the gates read
  the converted text. Every autofix call site (the attempt loop, FAQ regeneration, the first-line rewrite) gets
  it for free.
- **What counts as a dash:** an em-dash, spaced or not, and an en-dash with a space on both sides. A bare
  en-dash (`3–5 days`, `1637–1638`) and a hyphen are never touched. An em-dash between two digits
  (`1637—1638`, `20,000 — 24,000`) becomes an en-dash range.
- **Rules (per sentence, left to right):**
  - a pair `— X —` is a parenthesis: `, X,`; brackets `(X)` when X has its own commas, or when `and` / `or`
    follows the pair (`The Je Khenpo (the Chief Abbot of Bhutan) and the Central Monk Body …`; commas would
    read as three people);
  - a single `… — X` becomes:
    - `, X` when X continues the sentence: a conjunction, `not`, a relative word, `rather than` /
      `instead of`, a linking -ing word (`meaning`, `making`, `giving` …), or an -ly / -ed word with no finite
      verb after it;
    - `. X` (capitalised) when X is a clause of its own (subject pronoun, or a finite verb in its first 5 words
      before any comma / relative word and after at most one article) **and** the text before the dash has a
      finite verb (or is a bare `Yes` / `No`);
    - otherwise `: X` (`, X` if the sentence already has a colon).
  - The plan said `: X` when X has no verb, `. X` otherwise. Reading the 730 dash sentences of r1–r5 showed
    that a full stop is only safe with both checks above, so the colon is the default: it is never
    ungrammatical, while a wrong full stop leaves a fragment and a wrong comma a comma splice.
- **Headings:** the dash becomes `: ` (then `, `). `## Day 3 — Uran Togoo` → `## Day 3: Uran Togoo`. The
  §3.1 writer rule bans em-dashes in headings too, and a colon is the house style for a heading subtitle.
- **Titles / quotes kept verbatim:** the TRIP name written verbatim (`Ho Chi Minh Trail — 14 Days`), and a
  quoted span (`"…"` / `“…”`) that is word for word in the seed, the Facts block or the trip name. Nothing else
  is protected: the seed's own moment labels (`Uran Togoo — trek into …`) are internal labels, not titles, so
  a copied label is converted like any other sentence.
- **Residue guard:** `EM_DASH_PATTERN` (an em-dash with the word on each side) is in
  `quality_gates._BANNED_PATTERNS_SEED`. A bare `—` would be useless: F2 exempts a match found verbatim in
  the source, and **every** seed has em-dashes in its own labels (`Place background (… itself — you may …)`,
  `Uran Togoo — trek …`), so the bare character would always be exempt. The word-dash-word phrase is exempt
  only when the source has that exact phrase. The em-dash violation is readable and quotes its sentence
  (`uses an em-dash ('water — cold'); use a comma, a colon or a full stop: '<sentence>'`) instead of printing
  the regex.
- **F2 source in `run_quality_gates` = atom text + CTA + full trip name.** `ensure_cta` puts the configured CTA
  in word for word after autofix, and `fix_dashes` keeps the trip name: both would otherwise trip the guard on
  every attempt. (This widens the carve-out for every banned pattern to the CTA and the trip name; both are
  configured text the piece is required / allowed to carry verbatim.)
- **Admin sentence path:** `sentence_repair.sanitize_replacement` (T9 piece suggest/apply and T2 tour-version
  suggest/apply) now runs `fix_dashes` without a context. Without this, the new blocking F2 em-dash check in
  `check_replacement` would refuse an admin's or the suggest call's sentence with a 422; converting it follows
  the existing rule "a deterministic fix runs after the last LLM step".
- **Code uses escapes** (`—`, `–`) so a search for the character finds only text.

## Tradeoffs

- **Colon-heavy output.** The colon is the safe fallback, so a piece that used 13 em-dashes will now have
  several colons. Grammatical in every case read, but the live panel (AI-tells ≥ 5.5) is what tells whether
  colon density becomes its own tell. The prompt rule should cut the dashes at the source first; the autofix is
  the net.
- **Heuristic verb detection, no POS tagger.** A small finite-verb list decides `. X` vs `: X`. Misses fall to
  the colon on purpose; the asymmetric cost is documented in `_dash_trailing`.
- **The guard is blocking F2 → full rewrite**, not a sentence repair (F2_banned_patterns is not in
  `sentence_repair._ELIGIBLE_GATES`). It goes in only because the offline check clears 100% (plan: ≥95%), so it
  should fire only on a dash added after autofix (the CTA and the trip name are exempt; a family caveat
  `add_family_caveats` copies from the seed is exempt as source text).
- **Not run after contrast-frame detection.** The contrast detection lives in the gates, which already read
  autofix output. The plan's alternative was taken instead: contrast-frame counts are unchanged on every run
  (r1 43/43, r2 35/35, r3 42/42, r4 35/35, r5 37/37), because the frame regexes accept `,;:` and the
  two-sentence `not X. It is Y.` shape.
