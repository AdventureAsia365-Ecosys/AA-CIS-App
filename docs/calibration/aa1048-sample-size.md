# Sample size per detector and class — claim labels (AA-1048, Quality W2, S272)

How many labelled claims a gate needs before it may hold content (root ADR 0020: "a gate may hold content only with
a passing measurement record"; audit critic F2: "size CL-1 from a sample-size table per row instead of 60 claims").
Generated by `python -m scripts.aa1048.cli sample-size --markdown` (`services/ground_truth/sample_size.py`); the unit
test `tests/unit/test_aa1048_measurement.py` keeps this table equal to the code.

## The bars

| Bar | Precision | Wilson-95 lower bound of precision | Labelled violations (n_pos, for recall) | Labeller agreement of the class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLM claim instrument (R1) | >= 0.90 | >= 0.80 | >= 20 | - |
| Deterministic detector | >= 0.90 | >= 0.75 | >= 15 | >= 0.7 (unknown does not pass) |

Precision and its lower bound are measured on **every fire** of the detector (the labelled claims it flags, of any
class: a gate in enforce mode holds on any fire, so a fire on an `ok` claim or on a violation of an unrelated class
is a false positive); recall on the labelled **violations** of the class. A class needs both. A record that sets
`passes` (and so mode `enforce`) is measured on the **held-out** split only; the train split is for exploration and
the label-budget rates below.

## Labelled fires needed

Conservative reading: at an observed precision p, the true positives are floor(p x n), so the table never credits a
detector with more precision than it showed. The audit's "about 47 flagged" for P 0.90 / LB 0.80 holds for a detector
that shows 0.92; one that shows exactly 0.90 needs 70.

| Bar | Precision bar | Lower-bound bar | n_pos bar | Observed precision | Labelled fires needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| llm_instrument | 0.90 | 0.80 | 20 | 0.90 | 70 |
| llm_instrument | 0.90 | 0.80 | 20 | 0.92 | 47 |
| llm_instrument | 0.90 | 0.80 | 20 | 0.95 | 33 |
| llm_instrument | 0.90 | 0.80 | 20 | 0.98 | 25 |
| llm_instrument | 0.90 | 0.80 | 20 | 1.00 | 16 |
| deterministic | 0.90 | 0.75 | 15 | 0.90 | 40 |

| deterministic | 0.90 | 0.75 | 15 | 0.98 | 19 |

## Turning it into a label budget

A first panel pass gives, per detector and class, the share of ALL labelled claims the detector fires on (fire_rate)
and the share that are violations of the class (violation_rate); measure them on the train split
(`cli detectors --split train`). Only a held-out measurement may pass, and the frozen split puts about 30% of the
objects there (`HELD_OUT_PCT` in `services/ground_truth/claims.py`), so the fires and violations the bar needs must
all be on the held-out side:

    held-out claims needed = max(fires needed / fire_rate, n_pos bar / violation_rate)
    claims to label        = held-out claims needed / HELD_OUT_PCT        (about 3.3x the held-out need)

Example: fire_rate 0.1, violation_rate 0.1, a deterministic detector at precision 1.00: 150 held-out claims
(12 fires, 15 violations), so 500 claims to label in total.

`python -m scripts.aa1048.cli sample-size --rates rates.json --budget N` prints both per row
(`held_out_claims_needed`, `claims_to_label`; the `rates.json` rows are the `fire_rate`, `violation_rate` and
`precision` columns of `cli detectors`), and marks every class whose total is over the budget **stays warn**: decision D11 gives about 2-3 hours of tech-lead adjudication, so a class whose
violations are rare stays warn-only through W5 and is said so up front.

Rows are pooled across writers when the detector does not depend on the writer (critic F2); `--per-writer` splits
them when it does.
